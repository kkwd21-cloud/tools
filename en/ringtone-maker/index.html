Use it on Android

  1. The saved file is in your Downloads folder. If you made it on a computer, move it to your phone.
  2. Open Settings → Sound (or Sound and vibration) → Ringtone.
  3. Tap "Add from files" or "+" and pick the saved file.
  4. Notification sounds work the same way. For alarms, open the Clock app and choose the sound.

Menu names differ slightly by phone and version. More detailed steps (Galaxy, KakaoTalk...) ↓

Use it on iPhone

iPhone cannot set a file as a ringtone directly. There are two ways.

  1. With a computer: drag the .m4r file onto your iPhone connected by USB (Finder on Mac, Apple Devices app or iTunes on Windows). Then pick it in Settings → Sounds and Haptics → Ringtone.
  2. Phone only: import the file into the free GarageBand app, then Share → Ringtone.

    How to install it on your phone (Galaxy, Pixel, iPhone, KakaoTalk)

    How to set the saved file as a ringtone, notification sound or alarm. Menu names can differ by phone and version.

    Samsung Galaxy: set a ringtone
    1. Move the saved file to your phone. Skip this if you made it on the phone. From a computer, sending it to yourself in a chat app or Google Drive is easiest.
    2. Open Settings → Sounds and vibration → Ringtone.
    3. Tap "+", pick the saved file and tap Done.
    4. Do the same under Notification sound. Set alarms in the Clock app.

    With two SIM cards you may need to set SIM 1 and SIM 2 separately.

    Google Pixel and other Android phones
    1. Move the file to your phone, then use the Files app to put it in the Ringtones folder (calls), Notifications folder (alerts) or Alarms folder (alarms) in internal storage. Create the folder if it is missing.
    2. Open Settings → Sound (or Sound and vibration) → Phone ringtone.
    3. Pick the file name you just added. If it is not listed, restart the phone once.

    Some phones also have "Add from files" in the menu, so you may not need the folder step.

    iPhone with the phone only (GarageBand)
    1. Install the free GarageBand app from the App Store.
    2. Keep the saved file in the Files app on your iPhone.
    3. Open GarageBand and start a new project. In the tracks view tap the loop button → Files → "Import items from the Files app" and place the file on a track.
    4. Go back to My Songs, touch and hold the project → Share → Ringtone → Export. If it is too long you may be asked to shorten it.
    5. After exporting, choose "Use sound as" → Standard ringtone. Or go to Settings → Sounds and Haptics → Ringtone and pick it.

    GarageBand screens vary by version. These steps are the common method and were not tested on a real iPhone.

    iPhone with a computer
    1. Save with the iPhone option on this page to get a .m4r file. If that fails an MP3 is saved - use the GarageBand method above.
    2. On a Mac, connect the iPhone by USB, open it in Finder and drag the .m4r file onto it. On Windows use the Apple Devices app (or iTunes) the same way.
    3. Go to Settings → Sounds and Haptics → Ringtone and pick the name you added.

    iPhone ringtones must be 40 seconds or less. The computer method has not been tested on a real device.

    Change the KakaoTalk notification sound
    1. Put the sound on your phone with the steps above (Notifications folder on Android).
    2. In KakaoTalk open More → Settings (gear) → Notifications and open the sound option.
    3. Pick the file you added.

    This can vary by KakaoTalk version. On iPhone KakaoTalk we believe only built-in sounds can be chosen (not verified).

    Change an alarm sound
    1. Open the Clock app → Alarm and open the alarm you want to change.
    2. Tap the sound and pick your file. Galaxy uses "+" to add a file. On iPhone the file appears under Ringtones at the top of the sound list.
    3. Make the file with "Alarm" on this page. It fades in gradually.
    Different ringtones for different people
    1. Open the person in the Contacts app.
    2. Tap Edit → Ringtone and pick your file.
    3. Make each from a different part of the song so you can tell who is calling by ear.

    When to use it

    • Use a favorite song as a ringtone

      It cuts the most energetic part so sound starts right away, not after the call has already ended.

    • Use your own AI-made song

      Turn the chorus of a song you made into a ringtone, alert and alarm in seconds.

    • Make your whole phone match one song

      Calls, messages, chat, alarm and timer each come from a different part of the same song, packed in one ZIP. Your own AI-made song becomes your phone theme.

    • Different tones for different people

      Make several sounds from different parts so you can tell who is calling by ear.

    Tips

    • 20–30 seconds works well for ringtones

      Too long and the call ends first; too short and you may miss it. A part that makes sound from the first beat works best.

    • 2–5 seconds for notifications

      Message sounds are clearest when short and sharp. We choose a part with a clear attack automatically.

    • Alarms that fade in

      An alarm that gets louder gradually is less jarring. It fades in over 4 seconds by default.

    • Always listen on the phone speaker

      Earbuds and computer speakers sound very different from a phone speaker. Tap Phone speaker to hear how weak bass changes the sound.

    • Try Ring like a real call

      Hearing it ring, pause and ring again shows whether the start and end feel awkward.

    FAQ

    I do not see the Send to phone button.

    It only appears in browsers that support sharing files (Chrome and Safari on phones, Edge and Chrome on Windows). If you do not see it, use "Get all 6 at once" to download the ZIP and move it to your phone.

    How long should a ringtone be?

    About 20–30 seconds for calls, 2–5 seconds for message sounds and around 30 seconds for alarms. iPhone ringtones must be 40 seconds or less.

    Can I set it as an iPhone ringtone directly?

    No. iPhone cannot set a file as a ringtone directly. You need to move it with a computer or export it as a ringtone from the GarageBand app. The steps are shown on the page.

    The M4R file is not created.

    M4R can only be made when your browser supports it. Otherwise an MP3 is saved - use it with the GarageBand method. This depends on the device and has not been checked on every device.

    I do not like the part it picked.

    Tap Another part to see the next candidate. If that is not right either, use Pro mode and drag on the waveform.

    What is the noise test?

    It plays your ringtone as heard on a phone speaker over subway, cafe or street noise. If it may get lost it tells you, and Make clearer boosts the range human ears hear best. It does not raise the phone volume, and the noise is an approximation.

    How is the wake-up alarm different?

    A normal alarm repeats one part, but the wake-up alarm picks a section that starts quiet and builds up, so you wake up gently as the song unfolds. It keeps the song dynamics and skips the sound-flattening boost.

    What is the theme pack?

    It makes six sounds from one song - ringtone, message, chat alert, alarm, wake-up alarm and timer - each from a different part, and packs them in one ZIP. Each file name says what it is for and a short instruction file is included.

    What sound effects are there?

    Mood presets (dawn, cafe, game start, cosmos, old radio, concert hall), small room / big hall / cathedral reverb, dreamy, echo, warm, bright, heavy bass, telephone, lo-fi, 8-bit, chorus, phaser, flanger, tremolo and reversed. You can set each of the ringtone, alert and alarm separately, and switch back to No effect any time.

    Is the phone speaker preview exact?

    It is an approximation. Speakers differ by model, so the real sound may differ a little.

    Can I use someone else is song?

    Songs you made or AI songs you generated are the safest. For other songs, check the terms of use.

    Is my song uploaded?

    No. This is a static page with no server, so your song is never sent anywhere.
